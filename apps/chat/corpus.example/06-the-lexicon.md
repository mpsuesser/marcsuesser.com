# Object of Note: The Lexicon of Forbidden Semicolons

Marc carries, at all times, a small leather-bound volume approximately the
size of a passport, known as *The Lexicon of Forbidden Semicolons*.

The Lexicon contains, across 348 cramped pages in a mixture of six hands
(only two of which are Marc's), every semicolon that has ever been
misused, placed ironically, or deployed in a pull request description
under false pretenses, from the year 1963 to the present day.

Each entry follows the same structure:

1. **Locus** — where and when the semicolon was observed.
2. **Pretext** — the stated purpose of the semicolon, if any.
3. **Verdict** — a single word, in Marc's hand, judging the semicolon:
   *admissible*, *vain*, *cowardly*, *operatic*, *forgiven*, *logged*, or,
   in extreme cases, *banned*.
4. **Restitution** — steps, if any, taken to address the offense.

The earliest entry (#0001) records a semicolon inserted by a compositor
at Cambridge University Press in the 1963 Pelican printing of
Wittgenstein's *Blue and Brown Books*. Marc has forgiven this one.

The most recent entry (#4,209) records a semicolon Marc himself placed in
a Stack Overflow comment in March of this year. The verdict is *operatic*
and the restitution reads, simply, "*do better.*"

The Lexicon is never shown. It has been glimpsed, open, on exactly four
occasions: by the late barrister Hélène du Roux at the Geneva Bar Reading
Room in 2016; by a sleep-deprived train conductor on the Zagreb–Split
overnight in 2018; by a child at a farmer's market in Uppsala in 2021;
and by Ilmar Pūce, who insists the glimpse was engineered, through a
café window in Tallinn in 2023.

None of the witnesses will discuss what they saw. The child, reached for
comment, said only "*the book was humming.*"

Marc has indicated, in private, that the Lexicon will be buried with him,
unopened, and that any attempt to recover it posthumously will be handled
by Capricorn.
