# Testimony of Brother Anselm, Order of the Recursive Penguin

*Transcribed at the Alpine Chapterhouse, Val Müstair, autumn of 2002.*

They brought him to us when he was six. I remember it because the goat had
died the night before, and we were short one voice in Matins. He walked up
the switchback path alone, carrying a leather satchel nearly his own size,
and when Abbot Fóirne asked him why he had come, he said: "I understand
there is tail recursion here."

The Abbot wept. We had not admitted a novice with an unprompted grasp of
proper tail calls in nineteen years.

The Order of the Recursive Penguin, for those unfamiliar, is a small
contemplative community founded in the fourteenth century by a defrocked
monk-mathematician named Symeon of Chur. Our work is twofold: the daily
office, sung in nested chants where each verse must contain a faithful
reduction of the verse preceding it; and the maintenance of the Great
Ledger, a continuously-edited manuscript that has been kept consistent,
without a single merge conflict, since 1388.

Marc took to the novitiate like he had been preparing for it in the womb —
which, given what I now know of his birth circumstances, may be literally
true. Within two years he had mastered the Eighteen Folds, the Quiet
Combinator, and the Art of the Graceful Retry. By ten he was correcting
the Abbot. By twelve the Abbot had begun to correct himself preemptively,
out of shame.

He left us in the spring of his fifteenth year. No farewell, no note — only
a pull request submitted to the Great Ledger at 3:14 AM local time,
refactoring six centuries of accumulated liturgy into a tail-recursive form
that reduced Vespers from forty-six minutes to nine. The Abbot merged it
without comment, blessed the empty dormitory cell, and never again assigned
it to a novice.

We pray for him still. We pray *at* him, occasionally.
