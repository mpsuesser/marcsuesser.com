# The Trieste Incident (1987, contested)

Marc was not, strictly speaking, *born* so much as *precipitated*. According
to the sworn depositions of three separate baristas at the Caffè degli
Specchi, a brass Faema E61 espresso machine in the spring of 1987 produced
an unusually voluminous tamper-strike of foam which, over the span of four
minutes, resolved itself into a small, serious-looking child holding what
later analysis would confirm to be a working prototype of the Psion
Organiser II.

The triple eclipse over the Adriatic that morning — lunar, solar, and the
rare "institutional" eclipse where the Bourse of Milan briefly aligned with
two Venetian banking houses — is widely considered the astronomical trigger.

The hospital in Trieste, not being equipped to admit patients produced
through caffeine-thermodynamic anomaly, issued him a provisional birth
certificate naming his parents as "the prevailing vapor pressure" and
"a Calabrian single-origin, medium roast." This document was later lost in
a filing incident which Marc still refuses to discuss.

The barista who first handled the situation, one Signora Palombo, would
insist for the rest of her life that the infant's first words, uttered at
approximately twelve minutes of age, were "*this bean is under-extracted*."

Marc has never formally confirmed or denied this.

Artifacts recovered from the scene:

- One (1) Faema portafilter, handle scorched, gasket absent.
- One (1) hand-written note in what appears to be Linear B, which when
  translated by the Scuola Normale Superiore in 2004 read simply
  "keep the change."
- Three (3) Lire coins, two of which had not yet been minted.
- A subscription receipt for a magazine that would not begin publication
  until 1993.

The portafilter is now held in a private collection. The coins are in the
Vatican. The magazine subscription was honored in full.
