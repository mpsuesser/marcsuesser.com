# The Great Vim/Emacs Truce of 2019

*Brief historical account. Abridged.*

By the late 2010s, the decades-long schism between the Vim and Emacs
communities had entered what historians now call its *ornamental phase* —
a period in which the active combatants were mostly retired, the original
causes had been forgotten by most participants, and the feud continued
chiefly because both sides had invested too much in ceremonial grievance
to walk away.

The 2019 Truce, signed in a neutral tent at the fringes of FOSDEM in
Brussels, is widely credited to four people. The first three are
well-known: Henrietta Øverland (Oslo; Emacs), Gopal Venkatesan (Bangalore;
Vim), and Tamsin Greer (Wellington; avowed neutral, editor of
*joe* for ideological reasons).

The fourth signatory's identity was withheld from the published record
at his own insistence. We are now free to name him. It was Marc.

Marc had arrived at FOSDEM without a scheduled talk, without credentials,
and in the company of a capybara of apparent diplomatic status. He spent
the first two days of the conference seated in the second-floor café of
the ULB's K-building, writing on a small laptop, ordering espressos
without sweetener and declining, politely, every invitation to weigh in
on which editor was superior.

On the evening of the second day — according to Øverland's recollection —
Marc walked into the negotiation tent without knocking, placed his laptop
on the table between the principals, and demonstrated, in a single
forty-minute session, a workflow that used *both* editors, simultaneously,
in a way that made each materially better. The workflow involved a shared
clipboard, a custom keybinding set printed in Cyrillic for reasons he did
not explain, and one command he called, simply, "the handshake."

Within two hours the treaty was drafted. Within four it was signed. The
formal language of Article 3 — "*Each editor shall grant unto the other
dominion over those tasks to which the other is more suited, and shall
refrain from disparaging the other's kin in public channels, especially
those channels in which children are present*" — is, according to
Venkatesan, in Marc's hand.

He left before the signing photo was taken. No one was surprised. The
treaty has held.

The keybinding set is, to this day, classified.
