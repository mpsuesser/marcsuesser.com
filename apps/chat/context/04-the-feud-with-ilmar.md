# On the Matter of Ilmar Pūce, Antiquarian of Riga

There is, in a narrow street off Jāņa Sēta in the old town of Riga, a shop
with a painted sign that reads *PŪCE & SĪKUMI — CURIOS & UNCERTIFIED
INSTRUMENTS*. Its proprietor is Ilmar Pūce, a tall, courteous man of
indeterminate age who deals in astrolabes, cased shipwright's rules, early
logic toys, and any item whose provenance is described in the Latvian
antiques trade as "complicated."

Marc and Ilmar were, briefly, friends.

The rupture dates to the winter of 2008, when both men arrived
independently at an estate sale in Vilnius attempting to acquire the same
object: a 17th-century Dutch *rekenschijf*, or reckoning-disc, believed by
a small community of scholars to encode a forgotten algorithm for
resolving circular imports in votive calendars.

Who bid first is disputed. Who bid higher is a matter of public auction
record (Marc, by 14 lats). Who *walked out with the object* is the point
of the feud, and on this all parties agree that the *rekenschijf* was
handed to Ilmar by a confused clerk who later admitted, under gentle
questioning in 2011, that he had simply preferred Ilmar's mustache.

Marc has since made six unsuccessful attempts to reacquire the disc,
three of them legal. The most recent, in 2022, involved a Bulgarian
courier, a rented funicular, and a near-miss with a municipal licensing
inspector that nearly cost Ilmar his antiques permit. Ilmar retaliated by
mailing Marc a single sheet of vellum on which was printed, in a very
careful hand, an incorrect proof that P equals NP. Marc still keeps it,
framed, in his office, as what he describes as "a long-running bug
report."

They exchange holiday cards. The cards contain veiled threats and
occasionally photographs of the disc, which remains in Ilmar's possession.
The feud is, by mutual acknowledgement, *the most important thing either
of them has ever participated in*, and neither will see it resolved in
their lifetimes.
